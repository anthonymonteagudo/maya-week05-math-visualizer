    Positions and displacements

    A point tells you where. A displacement tells you how far and in which direction to move. S, E and W are points. E connects two arrows; it is not necessarily their midpoint.

    S = (0,0,0)   E = (3,4,0)   W = (6,0,0)

    Formula → substitution → result

    a = E − S = (3−0, 4−0, 0−0) = (3,4,0)

    b = W − E = (6−3, 0−4, 0−0) = (3,−4,0)

    a + b = (6,0,0) = W − S

    Look in the viewport

    Teal runs S→E; gold runs E→W; green joins S→W. The green arrow is the sum. Coordinates are world-space centimeters.

    Try it

    Move E while leaving S and W fixed. Each segment changes, but their sum stays W−S. Translate all points together: world positions change; displacement vectors do not.

    Length and unit direction

    Length answers “how far?” Normalization keeps only direction.

    Calculate

    a=(3,4,0)

    |a| = √(3²+4²+0²) = 5 cm

    u = a/|a| = (0.6,0.8,0); |u|=1

    Look and try

    The blue unit arrow has the teal arrow’s direction. Move E twice as far from S: length doubles; unit direction stays the same. Put E on S: length becomes zero and normalization is undefined. The tool hides the undefined arrow rather than inventing a direction.

    Use a unit direction for aim or movement; multiply by speed and elapsed time to obtain a displacement.

    Dot product versus projection

    A dot product returns a scalar. A projection can use that scalar to build a vector and a point. Do not give all three the same name.

    Segment alignment

    a·b = 3×3 + 4×(−4) + 0×0 = −7

    cos θ = −7/(5×5) = −0.28; θ ≈ 106.26°

    This is the angle between the arrow directions. The interior elbow angle is 180°−θ, approximately 73.74°.

    Project E onto the S–W line

    g = W−S = (6,0,0); u = g/|g| = (1,0,0)

    k = a·u = 3 cm

    H = S + p = S + k u = (3,0,0)

    p = H−S = (3,0,0) is the purple projected displacement.

    r = E−H = (0,4,0) is the pink perpendicular residual.

    Try it

    Move E across the guide. Watch the residual reverse. H can lie beyond S or W: this is an infinite-line projection, not a clamped segment. If S=W, the guide direction is undefined.

    Cross product: a perpendicular vector

    The result c is perpendicular to a and b. Its magnitude measures parallelogram area; normalizing c produces a direction n. Colors distinguish the two operands; axis letters identify the output component.

    Formula first

    c = a × b

    cₓ = aᵧb_z − a_zbᵧ
    cᵧ = a_zbₓ − aₓb_z
    c_z = aₓbᵧ − aᵧbₓ

    Then substitute

    a=(3,4,0), b=(3,−4,0)

    cₓ = 4×0 − 0×(−4) = 0
    cᵧ = 0×3 − 3×0 = 0
    c_z = 3×(−4) − 4×3 = −24

    c=(0,0,−24); |c|=24 cm²

    n=c/|c|=(0,0,−1)

    Look and try

    The normal arrow is drawn 3× longer only for readability. It is not the elbow pole: the normal is perpendicular to the bend plane, while the pole helps choose that plane. Swap a and b: c reverses. Make them parallel: c=0 and a unique unit normal does not exist.

    IK, FK and the bend plane

    IK: move the wrist target to choose the end position. Move the pink pole to choose the bend plane. The elbow-to-pole guide is a relationship line, not the cross-product normal.

    FK: rotate the shoulder, elbow and wrist in order. The wrist rotates the hand without changing the two bone lengths. Switching modes matches the current pose.

    Reach

    L₁=5 cm, L₂=5 cm. Reachable target distance r satisfies |L₁−L₂| ≤ r ≤ L₁+L₂, so 0 ≤ r ≤ 10 cm.

    Try it

    Move the target beyond 10 cm. The chain cannot reach it without stretching. Bring it back, then move only the pole. Observe that choosing an end position is not enough to choose a bend plane.

    Holding a prop: what does a constraint drive?

    The prop starts immediately beneath the palm. Its local axes show orientation; its label names the active link. Switching links preserves the current pose by recording an offset.

    Predict, then test

    Choose Orient and move the wrist. The prop need not stay in the hand because position is independent. Choose Parent constraint for a held prop. To change its offset, detach to World, edit, then relink.

    The math panel reports native constraint offsets and evaluated outputs. It does not claim that adding Euler angles reproduces Maya’s full orientation solver.

    Animate between two orientations

    t is progress: 0=start, 0.5=halfway, 1=end. The separated reference boxes show endpoint orientations; their spacing is not position interpolation.

    Euler LERP

    e(t)=(1−t)e₀+t e₁. For XYZ endpoints (0,0,0) and (90,90,0), t=0.5 gives (45,45,0) degrees.

    Quaternion SLERP

    q=(x,y,z,w) is a unit quaternion. xyz=axis×sin(angle/2); w=cos(angle/2). This w is neither a point named W nor a homogeneous coordinate.

    ω=acos(q₀·q₁). q(t)=sin((1−t)ω)/sin(ω) q₀ + sin(tω)/sin(ω) q₁.

    For these endpoints, q₀=(0,0,0,1), q₁=(0.5,0.5,−0.5,0.5). At t=0.5, q≈(0.2887,0.2887,−0.2887,0.8660). The intermediate orientation differs from Euler LERP even though the endpoints agree.

    Play and compare

    Play preview sweeps t from 0 to 1 over four seconds. Compare each method. SLERP follows the shortest rotation arc at constant angular speed when t changes linearly. Manual rotation changes the actual pose; changing t reapplies the formula.

    Bake for Maya playback

    Bake creates a separate pair of keyed boxes: Euler on the left, SLERP on the right. Use the main Maya viewport and timeline. Samples are exact at keyed frames; linear Euler curves between samples approximate SLERP. The take survives closing the tool and can be saved with the scene. Reset lesson does not delete separate takes.

    One point, different coordinate frames

    Local coordinates describe a point relative to an object. World coordinates describe that same point relative to the scene frame.

    Maya’s row-vector convention

    p_world = p_local M_world

    For translation (10,20,30): (3,4,0,1)M=(13,24,30,1).

    A direction uses w=0: (3,4,0,0)M=(3,4,0,0). Translation has no effect on it.

    Try it

    Rotate the parent and inspect world components. Local geometry stays unchanged. A normal under nonuniform scale needs inverse-transpose handling; it is not generally transformed like an ordinary direction.
